# Turning Northgate's meet-me rooms into a fabric tenants pay for

## The short version

On our June 10 call your team described cross-connects that still take days of manual work, and tenants leaving for a third-party fabric the moment they need cloud connectivity. The path forward is to give Northgate its own interconnection layer, so tenants provision in minutes under your brand and the connectivity revenue stays with you. Based on what you shared, that is a recoverable margin line you are handing to a third party today. The next step is a 30-minute working session with your network and finance leads to size it against one facility.

## What we heard

- You want to move beyond competing on space and power, where margin is thin and every renewal is a price fight.
- Every cross-connect is still a project for your team, and tenants increasingly expect portal-speed provisioning.
- When a tenant needs AWS or Azure, you point them to a third-party fabric, and the relationship starts moving there with the next thing they buy.

## Why moving now beats waiting

Based on the cross-connect volume you described, the manual provisioning and the connectivity revenue routed to a third-party fabric look like a low-six-figure annual margin line leaving the building, and it compounds as more tenants go cloud-heavy. The cost here is not a new project; it is the revenue the current setup keeps handing away every quarter you wait.

## The use cases we mapped

| Use case | What it does | The payoff for you | Who it helps |
|---|---|---|---|
| Automated cross-connects | A 1RU device in your meet-me room provisions connections from a portal, no manual config | Turns days of work into minutes, and frees your engineers from ticket churn | Engineering, Ops |
| Cloud on-ramp under your brand | AWS and Azure connectivity your tenants buy from Northgate, not a third party | Recaptures the connectivity margin you route away today, on a fixed cost base | Finance |
| Multi-site interconnection | One fabric across your facilities, so a tenant in two sites is one provisioning step | Keeps the multi-site tenant instead of losing them at the facility edge | Finance, Engineering |

## Working through the open questions

**You asked how this is different from a third-party fabric.** The difference is ownership, not features. With a third-party fabric your tenant is on someone else's portal and invoice, building a relationship with them. This is carrier infrastructure you deploy in your own meet-me room, so the tenant, the margin, and the brand stay Northgate's. You can still reach their ports through it; you are the landlord, not their tenant.

**You flagged that you do not have a large network team.** That is the common case, and it is why this is built without routing protocols to run. No BGP, no MPLS to manage. To take the risk off the table, we would start with a single device pair in one facility and prove the provisioning and the margin before you commit anything further.

## What operators like you have seen

A regional colo operator we work with described it as building their own fabric without the multi-year development project, and another runs automated cross-connects where every connection used to be a manual ticket. The team behind the platform previously built Acme Packet and 128 Technology, so the architecture is proven, not a first attempt.

## What each stakeholder gets

- **Finance** A new recurring, higher-margin revenue line from connectivity you route away today, on a fixed cost base that gets more profitable as tenants attach.
- **Technical** Automated provisioning with no new protocols to manage, and visibility across paths, on a single 1RU device per facility.
- **Operations** One operating model across your sites; a new service is a config push, not a buildout.
