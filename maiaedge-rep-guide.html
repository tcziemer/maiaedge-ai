1 What Is This?

The maiaedge-ai repo is a toolkit of AI skills and context files that make Claude an expert on MaiaEdge's product, customer segments, messaging, CRM, and outreach. Instead of starting from scratch every conversation, Claude loads pre-built knowledge and follows proven workflows.

Skills

Tell Claude what to do. Each skill is a step-by-step workflow: "research this company," "write a cold email," "analyze call transcripts," "run CRM hygiene." You trigger them by asking Claude to do the thing the skill handles.

Context Files

Tell Claude what to know. Segment cheatsheets, messaging frameworks, HubSpot schemas, competitive positioning, proof points, terminology. Claude reads these before writing so it sounds like an industry peer, not a salesperson.

4 The SDR Pipeline — Batch Outreach Step by Step

When to use: You have a list of companies and contacts and need personalized 3-email sequences + LinkedIn messages at scale. This is the batch workflow.

Step 1: Open the Right Project

Use MaiaEdge Sales Outreach or General Assistant on Claude.ai. If using Cowork, install the maiaedge-sdr-pipeline plugin.

Step 2: Paste the Initial Prompt

Copy this exactly. Replace the yellow placeholders with your details. The more context you give upfront, the less you'll need to correct later.

INITIAL PROMPT — COPY AND PASTERun the SDR pipeline on the following contacts. Process in batches of 15. For each contact: 1. Pull all available data from HubSpot first (company record, contact record, notes, activity, deals, segment) 2. Check the activity gate -- if there's an active conversation within 14 days, skip the contact and note the reason 3. Do web research to verify the account brief and find recent news, expansions, or competitive signals 4. Fill any missing contact data (email, LinkedIn) with Apollo 5. Verify the segment matches what research shows -- if HubSpot says Fiber Operator but they're actually a colo, correct it 6. Select ONE company-specific angle before writing. The angle should be something happening at this company RIGHT NOW, not generic segment pain 7. Write a 3-email sequence + LinkedIn message. Research is fuel, not content. Don't state company facts back to the recipient. The email should sound like an industry peer, not a salesperson who googled them. 8. After writing all 15 contacts, run the copy strategist skill on the full batch. Rewrite any email that scores below 8/10 or has research display, role-addressing language, or a product-heavy value bridge. Append each completed batch to: [output-filename.xlsx] When done with this batch, prepare a final continuation prompt I can paste into a fresh chat to handle the next 15 contacts on the list. Sender: [Tim Lieto / Ken Cunningham / Timothy Ziemer] Segment: [Fiber Operator / Colocation / Network Operator / Neocloud / MSP] Subject line: [connect at itw? / or leave blank for default "[Company] provisioning"] Key rules: - No em dashes anywhere. Use periods or commas. - No credibility anchors (no Acme Packet, no 128 Technology) - No competitor names (say "third-party fabric" not "Megaport") - No customer names in emails (anonymize: "one fiber operator" not "Arvig") - Warm contacts (HubSpot activity within 90 days) get shorter, more casual emails. When to use: Single contact, not a batch. For one-off outreach or follow-ups.

Step 1: Ask Claude to Write

Check the activity gate -- make sure we're not in an active conversation 3. Do web research on the company (recent news, expansions, competitive moves) 4. Research the contact on LinkedIn (background, tenure, what they care about) 5. Pick ONE company-specific angle before writing Then write a 3-email sequence + LinkedIn message. Sender: [Tim Lieto / Ken Cunningham] Remember: research is fuel, not content. Don't state facts back to them. The email should sound like someone who spent 10 years in their industry, not a salesperson who read their website.

Real-World Example

EXAMPLE — INDIVIDUAL EMAILWrite a cold email to Patrick Collins, CRO at Accelecom. They're a fiber operator expanding into Eastern Kentucky. Before writing: 1. Pull Accelecom's company record and Patrick's contact record from HubSpot 2. Check the activity gate -- make sure we haven't emailed him recently 3. Web research: what's Accelecom doing right now? New builds, acquisitions, partnerships? 4. Research Patrick on LinkedIn -- how long has he been CRO, what's his background? 5. Pick ONE angle that matters to a CRO at a fiber operator in growth mode Then write a 3-email sequence + LinkedIn message. Sender: Tim Lieto The angle should focus on the problem Accelecom is dealing with, not the fact that they're expanding. "Expansion into Eastern Kentucky" is research. The email should name the PROBLEM that expansion creates (like: revenue sits idle between build-out and activation).

Step 2: QA with Copy Strategist

Always run copy strategist after. This is not optional.

QA PROMPT — RUN AFTER EVERY EMAILNow run the copy strategist on that email sequence. For each email: 1. Score it on a 10-point scale 2. Check for research display (company facts stated as standalone observations) 3. Check for role-addressing language ("At the CEO level...", "From a BD standpoint...") 4. Check the value bridge -- is it 1-2 sentences naming an outcome, or a product pitch? 5. Check that Email 2 is a genuinely different angle than Email 1 6. Check that Email 3 has exactly one CTA, not two Rewrite anything below 8/10. Show me the before and after with annotations explaining what changed and why.
Best practice: Whether you're writing 1 email or 100, the pattern is the same: write first, then QA. The copy strategist catches research display, wrong vocabulary, product-heavy bridges, and structural issues that are hard to spot yourself. Think of it as a second set of eyes that reads the email as the RECIPIENT, not the sender.

6 Call Intelligence — Finding PMF & Sharpening Messaging

Why this matters: Call transcripts and summaries are the ground truth. What prospects actually say on calls tells you whether your messaging is landing, where it's falling flat, and what they need that you're not addressing. Use this to sharpen messaging before your next outreach batch.

Open the Call Intelligence project on Claude.ai.

Messaging Alignment — Is Our Messaging Landing?

Compares what prospects actually say on calls vs. what our messaging framework says they should care about. Flags gaps, surfaces their language, tracks which value props get traction.

MESSAGING ALIGNMENT PROMPTRun messaging alignment analysis for [segment, e.g. "colocation"] calls from the last 90 days. For each call, compare what the prospect actually said against our messaging framework: 1. What pain points did they name? Do those match what our framework says they care about? 2. What language did they use? Is it the same vocabulary we use, or different? 3. Which of our value props got a positive reaction (resonance, excitement, "that's what we need")? 4. Which value props fell flat (objections, silence, "not a priority")? 5. Which proof points landed vs. got skepticism? 6. What did they ask about that we don't address in our messaging? Show me a comparison table: Our Framework Says X, Prospects Actually Say Y, Gap. Then give me specific recommendations for how to adjust our messaging based on the data.

Product-Market Fit -- Where Are We Winning?

Scores each segment by resonance-to-resistance ratio. Shows where MaiaEdge is landing strongest, where it's struggling, and what prospects want that we don't address.

PMF ANALYSIS PROMPTRun a product-market fit analysis across all segments from the last 90 days. For each segment, I want to see: 1. How many calls we've had 2. Resonance signals (prospect was excited, asked about next steps, pricing, POC) 3. Resistance signals (objections, "not a priority", "we already do this", skepticism) 4. The resonance-to-resistance ratio (higher = stronger PMF) 5. Top 3 things that are working in each segment 6. Top 3 things that are NOT working in each segment 7. Unmet needs -- what are prospects asking for that we don't address? Grade each segment A through D (A = 3:1+ resonance:resistance, D = more resistance than resonance). Then show me the "messaging delta" -- where our framework says one thing but calls reveal something different. End with your top 3 recommendations for where to focus next.

Contact Call History -- What Has This Person Said?

Pulls every call for a specific contact. Shows how their concerns evolved over time, what commitments were made, and relationship health.

CONTACT DEEP DIVE PROMPTPull the full call history for [Name] at [Company]. For each call, show me: - Date, duration, which rep was on the call - What topics were discussed - What pain points they expressed (in their words, not ours) - Any objections or concerns they raised - Competitive mentions (who else they're talking to) - Action items and whether they were completed Then give me the big picture: - How have their concerns evolved over time? What changed? - What commitments were made by both sides? Any that went stale? - What's the relationship health right now? (Active, cooling, going dark?) - What should we focus on in the next conversation?

8 Golden Rules — Always Remember

These are non-negotiable. Every email, every batch, every time.
#RuleWhat It Means
1Research is fuel, not decorationResearch picks the angle. The email names the problem. Company facts are NEVER stated back to the recipient. No "Your 50,000 route miles" or "Your expansion into Kentucky."
2Sound like a peer, not a salespersonNo "At the CEO level, I'd imagine..." No "From a BD standpoint..." Just state the problem directly. A peer says "the fiber buildout is moving, the question is how fast it starts paying for itself."
3Always run copy strategist after writingWhether 1 email or 100. Copy strategist catches research display, wrong vocabulary, product-heavy bridges, and structural issues. This is not optional.
4Check HubSpot first. Always.The activity gate prevents tone-deaf outreach. If someone's in an active conversation, don't cold email them. Claude checks this automatically.
5One angle per email. Commit to it.Don't stack three value props. Pick the ONE thing that matters most to this person at this company right now.
6Value bridge: 1-2 sentences maxThe MaiaEdge mention should name the OUTCOME ("We help operators close that gap") not the product features. Max 1 product term per email.
7No em dashes. No credibility anchors. No competitor names.Replace em dashes with periods or commas. No "Same team that built Acme Packet." Say "third-party fabric" not "Megaport."
8Warm contacts get different treatmentIf HubSpot shows activity within 90 days, the email should be shorter, more casual, with warm framing ("We've connected with a few folks at [Company]"). Never fake warmth if there's no activity.
