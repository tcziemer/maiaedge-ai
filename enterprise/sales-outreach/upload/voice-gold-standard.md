# Voice Gold Standard — The Write-Time Reference

> Created 2026-06-12 from the outbound retrospective (`outputs/outbound-retro/2026-06-11-campaign-retro.md` §8).
> **This is THE page a writer holds open while writing.** Imitate these exemplars; check the 8 hard bans; differ from your own batch. Every other rule file (receipts, gates, persona blocks, hygiene) runs BEFORE writing (pipeline) or AFTER writing (batch QA) — not during.

## How to use this file

1. Read the exemplars for the channel you're writing. Absorb the register, then write YOUR sentence for THIS contact.
2. **Never reuse an exemplar's phrasing verbatim.** These are calibration, not clip-art. Any 8-word sequence from this file appearing in output fails the Batch Fingerprint Gate (email-writing-rules.md). **That includes the give-close and craft-line examples themselves** — "show you the whole thing end to end" and "is the layer I work on" are registers to vary, not strings to ship. Vary WHAT the demo shows in their world (a path coming up live / a site joining the fabric / telemetry read against a trace) and rotate the identity register ("what I spend my days on," "fixing that hop is my whole job"). Yesterday's craft line is tomorrow's stamp if it isn't varied.
3. After the batch: run the Fingerprint Gate. If two of your own emails share a closer or an 8-gram, rewrite one.

## The 8 hard bans (write-time checklist — the complete list lives in email-writing-rules.md)

1. No em dashes, no colons, no dashes as punctuation (spaced hyphen, double hyphen, en dash) anywhere in subject or body. Hyphenated compounds (cross-connect, on-net, multi-state) are fine. A colon labels a reveal and a dash fakes a pause, and both read as written-by-tool. Restructure so the point arrives as a clause.
2. No research display (company facts/stats/names recited back; max ONE cited public signal in E1's opening two sentences).
3. No yes/no thought-question closes ("Is this something you've thought about?" went 0-for-28).
4. No generic vendor we-claims ("We help operators…", "We've been helping similar teams…" as a default).
5. No credibility anchors, no competitor names (campaign briefs may sanction a named market catalyst, e.g. Megaport-as-signal).
6. No sign-offs, no greeting filler, no flattery, no move-announcing transitions ("another angle on this," "one more thought," "quick thought," "worth a thought"). Don't narrate the move. Just say the thing.
7. No banned segment terms (segment lock; neocloud/Enterprise sovereignty rules).
8. Nothing your own batch already said the same way (closers, openers, mechanism sentences — vary them).

## The three moves of the craft structure

1. **Structural truth** of their world, competence credited. Category physics they live with, never a diagnosis of their company.
2. **Craft line**: the sender is a practitioner of one layer ("that handoff leg is the layer I work on") + ONE concrete mechanic in their vocabulary.
3. **ONE close, three classes** (the give IS a close — never stack it with a second ask):
   - **Give-close** (technical buyers): demo offer fused into a single ask — "Fifteen minutes and I can show you the whole thing end to end, whenever works on your side." Live demo ready and sanctioned. A trailing clause may soften scheduling, never add another ask.
   - **Soft call-ask statement** (general): "Happy to set up time if it's worth a look."
   - **Honest-reason close** (senior business buyers): "felt close enough to your patch to be worth a conversation."

   The account one-pager is NOT a cold-E1 give — post-engagement only (after a LinkedIn accept via thank-you DM, or after a reply). On any reply: propose three specific times.

## A. The LinkedIn bar (Cooper-flagged, 2026-06-11)

> **Sergio, you know better than most that every operator in an alliance brings its own network, so each integration tends to get engineered from scratch. The layer I work on lets partners plug in once and reuse it, which felt close enough to your patch to be worth connecting.**

*Why it works: structural truth credited to his expertise; craft identity instead of vendor claim; disarmingly honest reason for the contact; no ask needed.*

> **Matt, customers mostly reach GPU clouds over whatever path they can arrange, which means the product's last hop is best-effort no matter how good the compute is. Fixing that hop is what I spend my days on, and it felt close enough to your world that connecting made sense.**

*Why it works: the problem belongs to the category's physics, so it can't offend; "what I spend my days on" is a person with a craft, not a company with an offering.*

> **Jeffery, Megaport raising close to $600M to move into compute signals the network's becoming part of the AI product. Arc tunes GPUs to the limit, so the path into them is the next place performance leaks. We're working with other neoclouds on owning it. Open to connect?**

*Why it works: market catalyst calibrated to a commercial seat; their excellence is the premise ("tunes GPUs to the limit"), the gap is the NEXT place, forward-state; micro-ask.*

**What pulls technical founders: the demo.** DeepInfra's CEO: "We would like a real demo of what the product does." Riedel's CEO: "you've made me curious on how it works end to end." Make them want to SEE it; the give converts curiosity into the meeting.

## D. Anti-exemplars (the shapes that earned zero replies at scale)

> ✗ "The move into Superior and Duluth, ImOn's first Wisconsin and Minnesota markets, puts business customers in a footprint that doesn't touch the rest of the network… **We've been helping similar teams wrap the access they already deliver in deterministic Ethernet, a layer above the transport**… **Is this something you've thought about?**"

*Diagnosis from research + vendor claim with an abstract noun-stack + thought-quiz. 0 human replies on 584 contacts; colleagues received near-identical copies.*

> ✗ "I noticed [Company] has been expanding into new markets and I wanted to reach out because we help operators like you…" — *research display + brand voice; pre-2026 shape, stays dead.*

> ✗ Passive zero-ask E3: "Easy to reach me if that becomes a priority." — *gives the reader nothing to do. Every E3 carries exactly one actionable ask.*

## E. The warm bar — post-accept thank-you DMs (Cooper-validated, 2026-06-12)

*The calibration set for the `warm-follow-up` skill (ACCEPT class). These were actually sent after LinkedIn accepts. Same rule as every section: imitate the moves, never the words.*

> **Dattatray, thanks for connecting.**
> **Put together a short one-pager on the thing I raised, the network as a layer E2E can own and sell rather than just consume, a private on-ramp into your compute under your own brand that the enterprise team attaches to deals, with in-country delivery you can actually prove. No agenda, just thought it might be useful given how E2E is scaling across India and beyond. Happy to talk it through whenever the timing works.**

*Why it works: the one-pager rides as thinking ("on the thing I raised"), the connect angle is restated compressed and sharper, never pasted back; "in-country delivery you can actually prove" is the India sovereignty angle in his vocabulary; "No agenda" + timing-in-his-hands close is the pressure-off register.*

> **Matt, thanks for connecting. The cloud you came up in shipped a private on-ramp as part of the product. Most AI clouds haven't built theirs yet. Put some thinking together on what that could look like for Nscale, if its relevant would like to set a call with my co founder who designed it to get deeper into the architecture.**

*Why it works: a structural truth from HIS OWN history (the cloud he came up in) credits competence before naming the category gap; the escalation give (a call with the co-founder who designed it) is a named person with a real reason — earned by a senior technical seat and an architecture-grade angle.*

> **thanks for connecting Dagi. dropping a quick one-pager on how we think about the neocloud side of this. worth a look when you have a minute. open to walking through where it fits as the inference footprint scales, whenever the timing's right. Understand you'll likely be taking some time to settle in so no rush!**

*Why it works: lowercase register matches the thread; minimal framing on the one-pager; the close reads the PERSON (new in seat, settling in) not the pipeline — "no rush" to someone who just landed converts to goodwill, and goodwill converts later.*
